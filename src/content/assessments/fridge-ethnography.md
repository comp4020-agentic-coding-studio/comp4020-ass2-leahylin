## The brief

> Observe one refrigerator, in use, by the people who use it, for long enough
> that they forget you are looking. Then tell me what governs it.

Draws on **Module 2 — Micropolitics** (weeks 4–6). The frameworks are Douglas on
classification, Goffman on performance, and the power-mapping method from the
Fridge Archaeology tutorial.

The site can be your family's fridge, a share house's, a workplace's, or a
communal one in a building. Contested space leaves more evidence, so a share or
workplace fridge often produces sharper material — but a household of one is a
legitimate site, and some of the strongest submissions have been exactly that.
The occupants are past versions of you, and they had different plans.

What separates a strong submission from an adequate one is not how much you
observed. It is whether you found something you were not looking for and followed
it, rather than collecting evidence for a conclusion you already held in week 4.
The most common failure is a competent description with no argument — which is
precisely what the week 6 workshop exists to catch.

## What you submit

A PDF, 2500–3000 words:

- **The argument**, stated early. What governs this fridge, and how do you know?
- **The evidence**, from your own fieldwork.
- **The frameworks**, used rather than name-dropped. A paragraph saying "Douglas
  argues dirt is matter out of place" and then doing nothing with it is worth less
  than one that reclassifies something in your own crisper drawer.
- **An appendix** of field materials — power maps, photographs, notes, the
  archaeology drawing. Not counted in the word limit and not marked directly, but
  it is what makes the observation credible.

## Fieldwork and ethics

Your fieldwork window is weeks 4–6, and the assessment is due at the end of week
6 — **before** the break, not during it. Three weeks is enough for a pattern to
show; the break itself is not fieldwork time you can lean on.

Two absolute requirements:

- **Consent.** Anyone identifiable has agreed in advance, knowing it is
  coursework. If somebody declines, that fridge is not your site.
- **Anonymity.** No real names, and no photographs in which a person is
  identifiable. Anonymise the household, not only the individuals.

The awkward part of this assessment is that the interesting findings are usually
about people you live with. That is manageable, and it is better talked through
than around — email Simone Ravel promptly, whose answer to "is this too small a
thing to write about" is almost always no.

**Do not tidy the fridge before you observe it.** Every year somebody does, and it
costs them the best material they had.
