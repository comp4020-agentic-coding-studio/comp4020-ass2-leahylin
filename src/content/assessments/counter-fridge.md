## The brief

> Design the thing that breaks the fortress. An appliance, a space, an
> installation, a protocol, a behavioural experiment, or a refusal. Then defend
> it.

Draws on **Module 4 — Resistance and reconstruction** (weeks 10–12). The question
is the last line of the week 10 lecture outline: could any version of this
technology serve the household instead?

The medium is open. Three shapes work well:

- **Spatial design** — a redesigned interior, a community pantry blueprint, a
  kitchen that assumes less cold
- **Art installation** — speculative or critical design, including designs meant
  to be uncomfortable
- **Behavioural experiment report** — run something on yourself or your household
  for several weeks and report it honestly, including if it failed

Week 10 built the maximally exploitative smart fridge, so you already know
precisely what a counter-design has to refuse. Week 11's ferments and pantry rules
are where many proposals actually start.

**A reasoned refusal is a valid submission.** If you conclude that households
should not be asked to solve a structural problem, and that the intervention
belongs at the level of retail or regulation, argue that. It is marked on the same
criteria.

## What makes it work

The weighting says most of it: **the argument is worth more than the artefact.** A
beautiful proposal with no case for it fails the same way a competent ethnography
with no argument fails — the failure this course warns about in weeks 6, 10 and
here.

Two questions your submission must answer explicitly, because the room will ask
them in week 12 if you do not:

- **Who is this for?** A counter-fridge that only works for someone who can shop
  three times a week and cook every night has answered a much easier question than
  the one asked. Week 11 was clear that de-refrigeration is a position most easily
  held by people with time and money.
- **What does it ask them to give up?** Every proposal trades something —
  convenience, reassurance, money, time, or the freedom not to think about it. Name
  the trade. A proposal that claims to cost nothing has not been thought through.

## What you submit

**The thing.** Any medium: drawings, a prototype, a document, a video, a protocol,
a rule set, a report. Photographs are fine if it is physical.

**The rationale**, 1000–1500 words: what it is, who it is for, what it costs them,
what in the course it refuses, and the strongest objection you have not answered.
Include that last one honestly — naming an unanswered objection reliably marks
higher than pretending there is none.

**The pitch**: five minutes at the [week 12 session](/sessions/12-unplugged/) —
the proposal, the argument, and that unanswered objection. Not slides.

Casper Nowak supervises this project and will tell you whether something is
buildable. Ask before you spend three weeks finding out.
