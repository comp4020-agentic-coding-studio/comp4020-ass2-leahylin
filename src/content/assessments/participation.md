## Why this is assessed

Almost every tutorial requires you to bring something from your own life:
advertisements in week 2, photographs of off-season food in week 3, a fridge
interior in week 4, a private position in week 5, a door and a feed in week 6,
5–10 date labels in week 7, a full week of waste records in week 8, honesty in
week 9, three inferences in week 10, a clean jar in week 11, and a pitch and a
dish in week 12.

None of that can be borrowed, and none of it can be done in the room. A tutorial
where half the group arrived empty is not a slightly worse class — it is a
different and much worse one, in which we discuss the reading instead of the
evidence, which is exactly what this course is trying not to be.

Hence ten per cent: small enough not to distort the marks, large enough to be
worth your Wednesday.

## How it is marked

Holistically, on the pattern across twelve weeks rather than any single session.

What counts:

- **Arriving with the week's material.** This is most of it.
- **Engaging with other people's.** Much of the course runs on comparison — pooled
  advertisements, the shared power map, group label forensics, paired drawings.
  Reading somebody else's fridge carefully is participation.
- **Consistency.** Twelve adequate weeks beat three brilliant ones and nine
  absences.

What does not count: talking the most.

## If you cannot attend

Email whoever is teaching that week in advance — Simone Ravel for weeks 1–6 and
10–12, Casper Nowak for weeks 7–9. Missing a tutorial with notice and doing the
preparation anyway is not penalised.

Two caveats. The **week 8 waste audit** cannot be made up later, because it depends
on records kept at the time. And the **week 1 blind tasting** cannot be repeated
once you know what the samples were.

If something is going on that will affect a stretch of the semester, tell one of us
early. We would much rather adjust than mark you down for it.
