## Why this is assessed

Almost [every tutorial](/sessions/) requires you to bring something from your own life:
advertisements in week 2, photographs of off-season food in week 3, a fridge
interior in week 4, a private position in week 5, a door and a feed in week 6,
5–10 date labels in week 7, a full week of waste records in week 8, honesty in
week 9, three inferences in week 10, a clean jar in week 11, and a pitch and a
dish in week 12.

None of that can be borrowed, and none of it can be done in the room. A tutorial
where half the group arrived empty is a different and much worse class — and
much worse to be in, whichever half you're on. Reading the assigned material
won't get you through it; this is exactly what this course is trying not to be.

Hence ten per cent: small enough not to distort the marks, large enough to be
worth your Wednesday.

## What counts

Holistically, on the pattern across twelve weeks rather than any single session:

- **Arriving with the week's material.** This is most of it.
- **Engaging with other people's.** Much of the course is on comparison — pooled
  advertisements, the shared power map, group label forensics, paired drawings.
  Reading somebody else's fridge carefully is participation.
- **Consistency.** Twelve adequate weeks beat three brilliant ones and nine
  absences.

## How it is marked

Assessed on whether you arrived with the week's material and engaged with
other people's, not on how much you spoke. A quiet student who consistently
brings their jar, their photographs and their audit marks higher than a
talkative one who rarely does.

## If you'll miss a session

Missing a tutorial with advance notice, and doing the preparation anyway, is
not penalised — email whoever teaches that week (see [People](/people/)).
Two exceptions can't be made up later regardless of notice: [the week 1 blind tasting](/sessions/01-artificial-cold/) and [the week 8 waste audit](/sessions/08-systemic-waste/). Full detail is on the
[Policies](/policies/#attendance-and-participation) page.
