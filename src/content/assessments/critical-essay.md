## The brief

> Pick one fridge-related phenomenon. Explain it, using the course, and then tell
> me what the course gets wrong about it.

Draws chiefly on **Module 3 — The tyranny of time** (weeks 7–9), where the
argument stops being interpretive and becomes measurable. The essay is marked
accordingly: on evidence and method as much as on reading.

**A phenomenon is smaller than a topic.** "Food waste" is a topic and will produce
a weak essay. "Why my flatmates and I each independently bought yoghurt on the
same Tuesday" is a phenomenon. So is a single advertisement from the week 2
workshop, one date label from Label Forensics, a single line item from your waste
audit, one household rule, or one of the drawings from week 9.

Date labelling is the most popular choice and therefore the hardest to say
anything new about. Choose it if you have an angle, not because it is nearest.

## What makes this essay work

**Method discipline is assessed.** Waste figures vary enormously with how they
were collected, and a global percentage quoted without its method is not evidence
— it is a rhetorical move. If you cite a number, say who measured it, how, and on
whom. This earns more marks here than theoretical fluency does. The same applies
to your own data: the week 8 audit is good evidence precisely because you know its
method, including where it is weak.

**Disagreement is welcome and is worth marks.** The course argues that the
refrigerator's promise of suspended time is real but irrelevant, and that the
waste it produces is its ordinary operation rather than its misuse. There are
serious objections: the medical and vaccine cold chain, reduced foodborne illness,
improved year-round nutrition, and the charge that blaming an appliance for
consumer capitalism is a category error. An essay that presses one of those well
will mark above an essay that agrees with the lectures fluently.

What will not do well is an essay with no position, or one that surveys three
views and declines to hold one. The course's own anti-neutrality applies to your
writing too.

## What you submit

A PDF, 2000–2500 words.

Your own tutorial material — the blind-tasting descriptions, the annotated
advertisements, the food-mileage traces, the power map, the honest labels, the
waste audit, the psychological maps — is legitimate primary evidence and is
usually the strongest thing in a submission. If you use another student's material,
you need their agreement and you anonymise them.
