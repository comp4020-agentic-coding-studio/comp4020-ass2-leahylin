**This week's question: if the fridge preserves, why does the household throw away
more than it did before?**

Inherited from week 7: the date label is the trigger. This week is the system the
trigger sits inside — costed, in your own kitchen.

## Before the session

**Keep a record for the seven days before this tutorial** of everything you
discard from your fridge. Start now; this cannot be reconstructed from memory, and
the attempt to reconstruct it is itself a demonstration of week 4's argument about
visibility.

For each item record:

- what it was, and roughly how much
- how old it was, or your best estimate
- **why** it went — spoiled, date passed, forgotten, cooked too much, went off
  before you got to it, no longer wanted
- what it cost, as best you can establish

Weighing is ideal. A daily photograph works if weighing is impractical.

If you throw out nothing in a week, record that too and bring your reasoning. A
genuine null result is interesting and rarer than people expect.

Read Bauman on consumerism and the Stuart household chapters.

## In the session

- **Costing the week** (30 min). Convert your records into a **monetary total** and
  a **caloric total**. We put both on the board anonymously. The money is the
  number that lands; the calories are the number that lingers, because they
  translate into how many days of eating you threw away.
- **Why it went** (25 min). Sorting the room's discards by reason. The categories
  are rarely "it spoiled." They are far more often *forgotten*, *bought too much*,
  or *date passed while still fine* — which are failures of attention and
  provisioning, not of refrigeration.
- **Bauman** (25 min). Liquid modernity and the illusion of abundance. If nothing
  is meant to be permanent and the consumer's job is to keep choosing, then a
  fridge full of half-commitments is not a malfunction — it is the system
  operating correctly. Test this against your own audit.
- **Relocation, not prevention** (15 min). For your three most expensive discards:
  what would have had to happen *at the point of purchase* for that item never to
  exist? The fridge moved the decision from the shop to your bin.

## Afterwards

Keep the audit. It is the strongest quantitative material most people generate in
this course, and it is directly usable in both the
[Critical Essay](/assessments/critical-essay/) and the
[Final Project](/assessments/counter-fridge/).

If the total was uncomfortable, that is the ordinary result and not a personal
failing — the entire argument of week 8 is that the number is structural. Say so
in the essay; do not apologise for it.
