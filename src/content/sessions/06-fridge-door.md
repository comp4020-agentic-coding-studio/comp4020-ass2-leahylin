**This week's question: what personas are we constructing in public digital space
versus semi-private physical space — and why are they different?**

_Module 2 — Micropolitics._

Inherited from week 5: the interior conceals, and concealment does classificatory
work. This week is about what the exterior displays instead.

## Before the session

Bring **two things**:

1. **A photograph of a fridge door** — yours, or one you have permission to use.
   The whole door, including the boring parts.
2. **Your own social feed** — Instagram, WeChat Moments, whichever you actually
   maintain. Scrolled back far enough to see a pattern.

You will only share what you choose to share; nobody is required to show a
personal feed to the room, and there is a paired alternative if you would rather
not.

Read Goffman's excerpts, including the chapter on performances.

## In the session

- **Reading the doors** (20 min). Photographs go up, and we read each other's
  households off them. People are startlingly accurate, which is the point: the
  door is legible, and it was meant to be.
- **Two curations** (30 min). In pairs, for each surface:
  - **Who is the audience?** The feed's is large, mostly weak ties, and partly
    imagined. The door's is small, strong ties, and physically present.
  - **What is the time horizon?** A post is optimised for the first 48 hours. A
    magnet stays up for nine years.
  - **What gets displayed on one and never the other?** The asymmetries are the
    finding — most people discover the door is more honest, more archival, and
    much less flattering.
- **The archive problem** (20 min). Expired coupons, invitations to events that
  already happened, a child's drawing by a child now at university. The door
  preserves too, and preserves differently from the interior behind it.
- **Ethnography workshop** (30 min). In threes. Not proofreading: each person
  states their argument in two sentences and the other two try to break it. A
  description with no argument is the most common way this assessment goes wrong.
