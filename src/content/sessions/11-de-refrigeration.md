**This week's question: can fermentation, ambient storage and shared provisioning
provide a real antidote — or only a comforting one?**

Inherited from week 10: the appliance extracts in exchange for reassurance. This
week asks how much of that reassurance was ever needed, and what could replace the
rest.

This is the practical tutorial, and the only one where you make something.

## Before the session

Read the Kallis excerpts on degrowth and sufficiency, plus the distributed
material on food sovereignty and alternative food networks.

**Bring a clean jar with a lid** if you are taking the fermentation stream. Around
500ml, washed in hot soapy water. Vegetables, salt and spices are supplied.

Choose your stream in advance:

## Stream A — quick ferments

Hands-on. We make quick pickles: salt, water, vegetables, time. You will take your
jar home and it will be ready in a few days.

The point is not the pickle. It is that preservation here **improves** the food
rather than suspending it, that it requires knowledge rather than electricity, and
that it returns a competence to the household instead of a subscription. Week 1
argued that this knowledge became unnecessary and then unavailable. This is what
reacquiring a piece of it actually feels like — about twenty minutes, as it turns
out.

Food safety is covered properly at the start: what makes a ferment safe, what
makes one fail, and how to tell the difference. This distinction is exactly the
week's larger argument in miniature.

## Stream B — the community pantry

Design work. In groups, produce the **operating rules and a spatial blueprint** for
a "Little Free Pantry" or community fridge, for a real site you can name — your
building's lobby, a campus corner, a street.

The hard parts are not spatial, and your rules have to address them:

- Who cleans it, and how often, and what happens when they stop?
- What may be donated, and what may not, and who enforces that?
- What happens to food that is nearly out of date?
- How do you keep it from becoming a dumping ground for what one household
  wouldn't eat?
- Who is liable if somebody gets sick?

A community fridge is a **redistribution of surplus sideways** rather than
downwards into a bin. It is also the point where degrowth stops being a theory and
becomes a roster.
