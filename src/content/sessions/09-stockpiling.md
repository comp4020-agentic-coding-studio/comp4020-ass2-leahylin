**This week's question: to what extent is consumer behaviour the purchase of
emotional value rather than use value?**

Inherited from week 8: the waste is structural and costed. This week asks why it
is comfortable.

## Before the session

Nothing to collect. Read Evans on anxiety and provisioning, and the distributed
papers on hoarding psychology.

Come willing to be honest. This is the tutorial people find most exposing, because
the material is not a fridge — it is you.

## In the session

Paper and pens supplied. Two drawings, and drawing badly is expected; nobody is
being assessed on draughtsmanship.

- **The ideal fridge** (15 min). Draw the inside of the fridge you would like to
  have. Not the one you think you should want — the one that would feel good to
  open. Label what is in it and how full it is.
- **The anxious fridge** (15 min). Now draw your fridge in a specific bad
  circumstance: while ill, during exam period, after a breakup, when broke, when
  the week has got away from you. Pick a real one and label it.
- **Sharing** (35 min). In groups of four. Two patterns come up almost every year,
  and it is better if you find them yourselves than if I list them here — but look
  carefully at **fullness** in both drawings, and at what kind of food each one
  contains.
- **Emotional value** (25 min). Working from the drawings: what were you buying?
  A stocked fridge as a defence against a sense of lost control; prepper
  psychology and pandemic panic buying as the same behaviour amplified; the empty
  fridge as an existential rather than a nutritional problem. Where did your
  personal threshold for "enough food in the house" come from, and have you ever
  revised it against your actual circumstances?
