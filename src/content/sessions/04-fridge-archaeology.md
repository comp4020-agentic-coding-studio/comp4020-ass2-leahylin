**This week's question: who holds the right to refrigerate — and how does vertical
space map onto household power?**

_Module 2 — Micropolitics._

Inherited from week 3: the cold chain fills the box. Now we ask who governs it
once it is full.

## Before the session

Bring the interior of a real refrigerator — your family's, your share house's, or
a communal one you have access to — as either a **photograph** or a **hand-drawn
map**.

Hand-drawn is better, and this is not a matter of taste. A photograph records
everything and shows nothing; drawing forces you to decide what matters, which is
the skill this module is teaching. If you draw it, mark the shelves, the door, the
drawers, and roughly what is where.

**Do not tidy first.** Every year somebody does, and it costs them the best
material they had.

If it is a shared fridge, tell the people you share with what it is for.

Read the Miller and Shove excerpts.

## In the session

We build one large power map on the board from everybody's fridges, marking three
kinds of territory:

- **Authority zones** — eye-level shelves, the front, the places whose contents
  belong to someone with the standing to claim them
- **Marginal zones** — the bottom drawer, the back of the middle shelf, wherever
  the health food and the least powerful housemate's groceries end up
- **Tragedy of the commons zones** — the unclaimed. Mouldy takeaway nobody
  ordered, condiments belonging to a person who moved out, the jar that predates
  the current tenancy

Then:

- **Archaeology** (30 min). Working oldest-first, like a real dig. For the oldest
  thing anybody brought: who put it there, why has nobody removed it, and who
  would have the standing to?
- **The three powers** (25 min). Procurement, distribution, disposal. In each
  household, do they sit with one person or several? What changes when they split?
- **Unwritten rules** (20 min). Pooling. Most rooms find the same four or five
  rules recurring across unrelated households, which raises the question of where a
  convention nobody transmits comes from.

## Afterwards

The [Fridge Ethnography](/assessments/fridge-ethnography/) opens today and is due
**Friday 2 April**, at the end of this module. That gives you three weeks, not an
afternoon — and the break afterwards is not fieldwork time you can lean on.
Today's map is a legitimate first artefact for it.

Settle two things this week rather than in week 6: whether the people you live
with consent to being written about, and whether you can observe without tidying.
The second is harder than it sounds.
