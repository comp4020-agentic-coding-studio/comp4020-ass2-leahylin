**This week's question: when does food become "leftovers," and when do leftovers
become rubbish? Neither transition is about the food.**

_Module 2 — Micropolitics._

Inherited from week 4: shelf position determines what is seen. This week is about
what being unseen does to a thing's category — and who gets to decide.

## The scenario

> Roommate A threw away food belonging to Roommate B without asking. It had been
> in the fridge for a week. It had no label. It looked edible.

Every element of that is load-bearing. A week is long enough to be arguable and
short enough to be contested. No label removes the marker that would have settled
ownership. *Looked edible* means the disgust judgement was A's alone, and B never
got to make it.

## Before the session

Read Douglas — the chapters on dirt as matter out of place and as the enemy of
order.

Then decide, privately, what you actually think, and write one sentence for it.
Keep it: you will very likely be assigned the other side, and comparing your
assigned argument with your real one afterwards is part of the exercise.

## In the session

- **Assignment and preparation** (15 min). Groups are assigned a position; you do
  not choose it. Positions include A's right to maintain shared space, B's
  property rights, and the position that the real failure was the absence of an
  agreed rule.
- **The debate** (40 min). Structured: opening, rebuttal, close. Three questions
  the adjudication turns on:
  - **Ownership.** Does food in a shared appliance remain wholly private property?
  - **Hygiene boundaries.** Whose disgust threshold governs shared space, and why
    should it be the most sensitive person's or the least?
  - **The contract.** Is there an implied roommate contract, and what did A and B
    each assume it said?
- **Douglas on the verdict** (20 min). The unlabelled container is an anomaly — it
  does not fit the classification system, and Douglas predicts exactly what
  happens to anomalies. Was the food thrown out because it was spoiled, or because
  it was *unclassifiable*?
- **Switching back** (15 min). Read out the sentence you wrote beforehand. Who
  moved, and what moved them?

## Afterwards

Nothing submitted. If this debate described your own house with uncomfortable
accuracy, that is common, and it is legitimate
[ethnography](/assessments/fridge-ethnography/) material — anonymised, and with
the consent of the people involved.
