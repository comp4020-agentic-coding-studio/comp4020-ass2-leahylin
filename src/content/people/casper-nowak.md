Casper Nowak spent eleven years weighing what households throw away. Not
surveying them — weighing. The gap between the two is most of the field: ask
people what they discard and they consistently report far less than a bin audit
finds, and they are not lying. They genuinely do not see it, because the fridge
is very good at converting food into something that was never quite thrown out,
only found too late.

Their current work is on date labelling, which is where the course's argument
stops being interpretive and becomes measurable. Two labels — *best before* and
*use by* — mean legally distinct things, are read as identical by most
shoppers, and the resulting confusion is a documented, quantified contributor
to household waste. It is the cleanest available case of a technical threshold
that reads as neutral and is nothing of the kind.

Nowak gives the lectures in weeks 7–9, including the week 7 deck, and
supervises the Counter-Fridge Design Project.
