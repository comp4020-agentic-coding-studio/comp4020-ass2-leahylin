Simone Ravel works on the material culture of domestic storage. Before this
course that meant a decade on what households did before mechanical cooling:
larders, cold rooms, salting, potting, the shared cellar, and the substantial
body of knowledge required to keep food edible without a compressor.

That work produced the argument this course is built on. The fridge's
achievement was never preservation — plenty of things preserved food before it.
Its achievement was **forgetting**: it made a demanding household competence
unnecessary, then made it unavailable, and then made its own necessity look
like a fact of nature rather than the outcome of a century of manufacturing,
electrification and advertising.

Ravel supervises the Fridge Ethnography, and is the person to ask when a
fieldwork question turns out to be an ethics question — which it usually does.
Disagreement with the course's central claim is welcome, and is worth marks in
the essay.
