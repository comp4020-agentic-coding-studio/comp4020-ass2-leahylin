_Module 4 — Resistance and reconstruction._

Having spent ten weeks on the diagnosis, this week is the constructive one, and it
has to be careful. The de-refrigeration position is easily discredited by
overreach — a vaccine cold chain and a domestic door shelf are not the same claim,
and conflating them is the standard way this argument gets dismissed.

The eco-Marxist and **degrowth** frame is what keeps it honest. The problem is not
the appliance; it is a mode of provisioning that requires continuous throughput,
in which the fridge is a component. Degrowth asks what a materially smaller
system that met needs better would look like, and refuses the assumption that less
throughput means less wellbeing. Applied here, the question is not "should we
abolish refrigeration" but "how much of this refrigeration is doing work for us,
and how much is doing work for the system that sold it?"

Four antidotes are worth taking seriously, each of which existed before the fridge
and none of which is nostalgia:

- **Fermentation and pickling** — preservation that improves food rather than
  suspending it, and that gives a household back a competence rather than a
  subscription.
- **Root cellars and ambient storage** — much of what we refrigerate does not
  require it, and different countries disagree sharply about which parts, which is
  the best evidence that the boundary is convention rather than physics.
- **Community fridges and shared pantries** — pooling storage so surplus moves
  sideways to someone who wants it instead of downwards into a bin.
- **On-demand local agriculture and alternative food networks** — shortening the
  chain so the cold chain has less to do.

Inherits from week 10: the appliance extracts in exchange for reassurance. This
week asks how much of that reassurance was ever needed, and what could replace the
rest.
