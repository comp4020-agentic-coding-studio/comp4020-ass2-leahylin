_Module 2 — Micropolitics._

Everything so far has happened behind a closed door. This week is about the door
itself: the only surface of the appliance that faces the room, and therefore the
only part that is curated.

Goffman's distinction between **front stage** and **back stage** lands here almost
too neatly. The interior is back stage — the household's actual consumption,
unmanaged, occasionally shameful, and closed. The exterior is front stage: magnets
from places somebody went, a child's drawing, an expired coupon, a wedding
invitation, a photograph, the schedule. Every item on it was chosen and left
there, and the choosing was done with an audience in mind.

The door is making a claim: **this is the kind of family we are**. It is addressed
to visitors, to the household itself, and to a slightly idealised observer who
never actually visits. And it is generally making that claim in direct
contradiction to the interior six centimetres behind it, which is the course's
argument in physical form. The door says who we are. The interior says what we
actually did.

The door is also an **archive**, and a badly maintained one — things stay up long
after they expire, which makes the exterior a record of a household's past
performances layered on top of each other.

Inherits from week 5: the interior conceals, and concealment does classificatory
work. This week is about what the exterior displays instead.

## Outline

- Goffman: front stage, back stage, impression management
- an inventory of the door: what is displayed, and to whom
- the audience that never visits
- the door as a badly curated archive
- interior and exterior read against each other
