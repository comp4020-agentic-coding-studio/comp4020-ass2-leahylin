_Module 2 — Micropolitics._

Douglas's formulation is the most useful sentence in the course: **dirt is matter
out of place**. Dirt is not a property of a substance. It is a judgement about
whether something is where the system of classification says it belongs, and it
tells you far more about the system than about the substance.

Apply it to a refrigerator and the results are immediate. The same rice, cooked at
the same time, is *meal prep* in a labelled glass container on the middle shelf
and *dangerous leftovers* in a knotted plastic bag at the back. The food is
identical. The container, the label, the position and the intention have changed —
and those are what determine whether it gets eaten or binned.

This produces the course's central mechanism in miniature. The refrigerator does
not resolve a food's status; it **defers** it. Putting something in the fridge is a
way of not deciding about it, and the deferred decision, when it finally arrives,
is very often the bin. Frugality and disgust turn out to be the same boundary
viewed from either side: eating the leftovers is virtue, and the exact same act
one day later is contamination.

Inherits from week 4: shelf position determines what is seen. This week is about
what being unseen does to a thing's category.

## Outline

- Douglas on dirt, anomaly, and classification as system-maintenance
- container, label and position as classificatory instruments
- meal prep versus leftovers: the same food, different verdicts
- deferral — the fridge as the place you put a decision you are avoiding
- frugality and disgust as one boundary from two sides
