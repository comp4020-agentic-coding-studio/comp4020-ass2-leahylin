_Module 3 — The tyranny of time._

Here is the fact the course exists to explain. Households with reliable
refrigeration waste more food, not less. The appliance whose entire purpose is
preventing spoilage is associated with more spoilage, and this is not a paradox or
a measurement artefact.

Bauman gives us the frame. In **liquid modernity**, consumption is not about
acquiring things you need and keeping them; it is a continuous, restless process
in which nothing is meant to be permanent and the consumer's job is to keep
choosing. Solid commitments dissolve. Applied to a kitchen, the fridge is the
appliance that makes food liquid in exactly this sense — always replaceable,
never quite committed to, held in a state of suspended decision.

The mechanism is straightforward once you have had seven weeks of it. Cheap
storage makes bulk purchase rational. Bulk purchase exceeds appetite. The excess
enters a box where it becomes invisible (week 4), category-deferred (week 5), and
governed by a date nobody understands (week 7). The fridge does not prevent the
waste. It **relocates** it — from the shop, where you would have had to decide
against buying, to your own bin three weeks later, where time has made the
decision for you and no one has to feel they chose it.

This is what "illusory fortress" means concretely. The fortress works. The food
really is colder and really does last longer. It simply does not matter, because
the binding constraint was never the rate of decay. It was **attention**.

Inherits from week 7: the date label is the trigger. This week is the system the
trigger sits inside.

## Outline

- the measured relationship between refrigeration capacity and household waste
- Bauman on liquid modernity: consumption as process rather than acquisition
- the illusion of abundance, and why a full fridge licenses more buying
- relocation rather than prevention — from the shop's bin to yours
- attention, not temperature, as the binding constraint

## Reading

- Zygmunt Bauman, _Liquid Modernity_ (2000) — the chapters on consumerism
- Tristram Stuart, _Waste_ (2009) — the household chapters

A caution on numbers, and it is assessed. Waste figures vary enormously with
method, and anyone quoting a single global percentage without saying how it was
measured is not being careful. Bring the method, not just the number.
