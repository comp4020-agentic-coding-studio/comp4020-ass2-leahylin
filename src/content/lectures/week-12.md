_Module 4 — Resistance and reconstruction._

The last lecture is short: **fifteen minutes**, because the rest of the week
belongs to your work rather than ours.

We review the twelve-week journey — from the disenchantment of manufactured cold,
through the micropolitics of the interior, through the tyranny of dates and the
comfort of fullness, to the question of what could be built instead — and restate
the thesis one final time, now that you have the evidence to argue with it.

**The refrigerator is not a neutral appliance. It is the control centre of
micropolitics in the modern household, and an illusory fortress against temporal
decay. By manufacturing expiration anxiety and hoarding illusions, it colludes
with and sustains late capitalism's logic of food waste and overconsumption.**

You should also leave holding the strongest objections, stated properly rather
than strawmanned: refrigeration has saved an enormous number of lives through the
vaccine and medical cold chain; it has materially improved year-round nutrition
and reduced foodborne illness; blaming an appliance for consumer capitalism may be
a category error; and de-refrigeration is a position most easily held by people
who can afford to shop three times a week. A student who can argue the course's
own case against it has understood it better than one who agrees fluently.

Then we stop talking and eat.

## The unplugged celebration

The course ends with a **refrigerator-independent gathering**: room-temperature
bread, fresh fruit, ambient drinks, ferments from last week's workshop, and
whatever else needs no cold to be good.

This is not a gimmick and it is not a stunt. It is the argument in embodied form —
a demonstration, lasting one afternoon, that a great deal of pleasure and
hospitality requires no fortress at all. Bring something that never saw the
inside of a fridge.
