_Module 4 — Resistance and reconstruction: towards a post-refrigerator era. The
module's objective is to explore how to break the illusory fortress, and to find
more ecologically conscious and agentic alternatives._

The IoT refrigerator is sold as the solution to precisely the problem this course
has spent nine weeks describing. It has cameras inside it. It knows what it
contains and how old each thing is. It will order replacements before you run out
and suggest what to cook. If the diagnosis in weeks 4, 5 and 8 was that the
binding constraint is attention rather than temperature, then an appliance that
supplies attention ought to fix it.

This week asks why it does not, and the answer is about who the inventory is for.

A camera inside a fridge generates a continuous, timestamped, itemised record of a
household's consumption. That is among the most commercially valuable datasets a
home can produce, and it is not produced for the home. From food alone a system
can infer household size, income band, dietary and religious observance,
pregnancy, illness, working hours, and whether somebody has recently moved out.
The interface shows you your spinach. The data shows somebody else your life.

Zuboff supplies the vocabulary: **behavioural surplus** is not a byproduct of the
service, it is the product, and the service is the lure. Layered on top is a
quieter thing — **health discipline**. An appliance that suggests what you should
eat is exercising a normative authority nobody voted to give it, in the most
intimate room in the house, on behalf of whoever configured it.

Inherits from week 9: the fridge sells reassurance. This week is about what it has
begun taking in exchange.

## Outline

- the household inventory as behavioural surplus
