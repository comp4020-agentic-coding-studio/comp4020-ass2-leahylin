_Module 3 — The tyranny of time: anxiety, illusions, and complicity with capital.
The module's objective is to strike at the core of the course, and deconstruct how
the refrigerator manipulates our perception of time and drives overconsumption._

**Best before** is a prediction about quality. **Use by** is a statement about
safety, applying to a narrow category of genuinely high-risk foods. They are
legally distinct, governed differently, and almost universally read as the same
thing: a deadline, printed by somebody who knows, after which food is dangerous.

The register of this course changes here. For six weeks the argument has been
interpretive — hierarchy, classification, performance. This week it becomes
**measurable**, because the confusion between these two labels is documented,
quantified, and traceable to specific commercial incentives.

Follow those incentives and the picture is not subtle. A conservative date limits
liability. It protects the brand from a customer who eats something stale and
blames the manufacturer rather than the calendar. It supports shelf-space
negotiation with retailers. It accelerates turnover, because a household that
discards food on schedule buys replacement food on schedule. At every step there
is a reason to print a date earlier than the food requires, and no step at which
anyone is paid to print it later.

So a number appears on a jar. It looks like a fact about the food. It is a fact
about liability, brand management and regulatory caution — and obeying it costs
households money and costs the world food.

Inherits from week 6: the household performs competence on the door. This week
asks who wrote the standard that competence is measured against.

## Outline

- what _best before_ and _use by_ mean in law, and the narrow high-risk category
- how the two were blurred, and who benefits from the blur
- systematically manufactured safety anxiety as a turnover mechanism
- liability, brand protection and compounding regulatory caution
- what a household would need to know to override a date, and why it doesn't
