# The tyranny of labels

SLOP8364 · Week 7 · Dr Casper Nowak

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## One of these is an instruction.

## The other is an opinion.

---

## Best before

A prediction about **quality**.

The manufacturer's estimate of when the product stops being at its best.

After this date, food is in the overwhelming majority of cases entirely
edible.

---

## Use by

A statement about **safety**.

Applies to a narrow category of genuinely high-risk foods.

This is the one to treat as an instruction.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Legally distinct. Governed differently.
> Read by almost everyone as the same thing:
> a deadline, printed by somebody who knows.

---

## Follow the incentive

Why print a date earlier than the food requires?

- limits **liability**
- protects the **brand** — a customer eating something stale blames the label's
  owner, not the calendar
- supports **shelf-space negotiation** with retailers
- accelerates **turnover** — discard on schedule, replace on schedule

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## At no step is anyone paid to print it later.

---

## The three questions

For any date, on any package:

1. Who wrote this number?
2. What were they protecting?
3. What happens to me if I ignore it?

The answers are rarely _a food scientist_, _my health_, and _illness_.

---

## What the date does not know

- how cold your fridge actually runs
- whether the packet has been opened
- how long it sat in a hot car
- what your nose is telling you right now

Your senses have information the printer never had.

---

## Manufactured anxiety

Ambiguity is not a failure of the labelling system.

It is a **feature** of it: safety anxiety, systematically produced, converts
directly into turnover.

The confusion is documented, quantified, and traceable.

---

## The competence that was lost

**Week 1** — households once knew how to judge whether food was good.

**Week 7** — that judgement is outsourced to a number printed by someone with
different incentives.

The date label is where the forgetting became infrastructure.

---

## Label forensics — today's tutorial

Sort your 5–10 items into:

- a **safety baseline**
- a **peak of flavour**
- a date that **should never have been printed**

Then draft an honest label for three of them.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> An honest label has to say what it knows,
> what it does not know,
> and what you should actually check.
