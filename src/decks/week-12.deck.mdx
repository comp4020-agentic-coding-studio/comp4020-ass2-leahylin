{/* _class: section-break */}

<div className="thesis-quote">

<span className="eyebrow">The thesis, in its final form</span>

> The refrigerator is not a neutral appliance. It is the **control centre of
> micropolitics** in the modern household, and an **illusory fortress**
> against temporal decay. By manufacturing expiration anxiety and hoarding
> illusions, it **colludes with and sustains** late capitalism's logic of
> food waste and overconsumption.

</div>

```notes
**~2 min** (0:06)

The thesis in its final form. Read it slowly and completely — it is the only
slide today that earns being read aloud word for word.

Then unpack in one pass, naming where each clause was earned:

- **control centre of micropolitics** — weeks 4, 5, 6.
- **illusory fortress** — week 8. And be precise about why it is illusory:
  the fortress *works*, the food really is colder, and it does not matter,
  because the binding constraint was never the rate of decay. It was
  attention.
- **manufacturing expiration anxiety** — week 7.
- **hoarding illusions** — week 9.
- **colludes with and sustains** — deliberately not "causes." The appliance
  is a component, not a culprit. Students who read it as "the fridge is to
  blame" have the weakest version of this argument, and they write the
  weakest essays on it.

Then say the crucial thing about its status: **this is a claim, and you now
have the evidence to argue with it.** In week 1 they could only accept or
reject it. Today they can test it. That difference is the course.
```

## The four strongest objections

Stated properly, not strawmanned.

<hr />

```notes
**~30 sec** (0:08)

Signal what this section is for.

You are marking a Critical Essay and forty Final Projects in a fortnight. The
single best predictor of a strong mark is whether the student can state the
case against their own position without flinching. So this section is not
balance for its own sake — it is the assessment criterion, made explicit.

Halfway point. If you are behind, **this is the section to compress**: put
all four on the board, name them in a sentence each, and move.
```

<div className="objection">
  <b>1</b>
  <div>
    <h2>The medical cold chain</h2>
    <p className="lead">Refrigeration has saved an <strong>enormous</strong> number of lives.</p>
    <div className="chips" style={{ flexWrap: "wrap" }}>
      <span>Vaccines</span>
      <span>Insulin</span>
      <span>Blood</span>
      <span>Antivenom</span>
      <span style={{ background: "none", fontWeight: 400 }}>Transplant organs</span>
    </div>
    <p>None of that is a door shelf, and conflating them is how this argument gets dismissed.</p>
  </div>
</div>

```notes
**~1 min** (0:09)

The objection with the highest body count on its side, so lead with it and
concede it fully.

Week 11 already drew the line: a vaccine cold chain and a domestic door shelf
are not the same claim. The de-refrigeration position discredits itself the
moment it stops distinguishing them, and it does so often enough in public
that the distinction has to be made out loud every time.

What survives the concession: the course's object was always the **domestic**
appliance and the provisioning system around it. That is a real scope
limitation, not a dodge — say it as a limitation, and then hold students to
the same honesty about the scope of their own proposals.
```

<div className="slide-head"><span>Objection 2 of 4</span><span>SLOP8364 · WK 12</span></div>

<div className="objection">
  <b>2</b>
  <div>
    <h2>Public health</h2>
    <div className="rule-cards" style={{ "--cols": 2 }}>
      <div><b>Reduced foodborne illness</b><p>A category of ordinary illness made rarer.</p></div>
      <div><b>Year-round nutrition</b><p>The seasonal nutrition gap, flattened.</p></div>
    </div>
    <p>Safe milk, safe meat, safe leftovers, in households that would otherwise be choosing between risk and hunger.</p>
    <p className="statement" style={{ fontSize: "1.625rem" }}>The households that benefit most are the poorer ones. An argument that treats the fridge as a luxury has assumed an affluent household.</p>
  </div>
</div>

```notes
**~1 min** (0:10)

The objection students most often skip, and the one that most deserves a
hearing.

Refrigeration did not only redistribute food. It made a category of ordinary
illness rarer, and it flattened the seasonal nutrition gap that used to be a
real constraint on health. Both are large, both well evidenced, and neither is
undone by anything in this course.

The distributional point matters and it cuts against us: the households that
benefit most from reliable refrigeration are **poorer** households — further
from shops, buying in bulk because bulk is cheaper, with less slack for a
wasted trip. Any argument that treats the fridge as a luxury to be given up
has quietly assumed an affluent household.
```

---

<div className="slide-head"><span>Objection 3 of 4</span><span>SLOP8364 · WK 12</span></div>

<div className="objection">
  <b>3</b>
  <div>
    <h2>The category error</h2>
    <p className="lead">Blaming an <strong>appliance</strong> for consumer capitalism may be a category error.</p>
    <p>The throughput logic predates the fridge and would survive its removal. Remove every fridge tomorrow and the logic finds another instrument.</p>
    <p className="statement" style={{ fontSize: "1.625rem", borderInlineStartColor: "#000" }}>The reply, and it is narrow: the appliance is where the system becomes a household decision. That makes it the right <strong>site</strong> — not the cause.</p>
  </div>
</div>

```notes
**~1 min** (0:11)

The sharpest objection intellectually, and the one a good essay attacks this
course with. Put it at full strength.

If the driver is a mode of provisioning that requires continuous throughput,
then the refrigerator is a symptom wearing the costume of a cause — and a
course that spends twelve weeks on the appliance has mistaken the visible
thing for the operative one.

The honest reply is narrower than students expect, so give it in those terms:
the appliance is where the system becomes a household **decision** — the
point at which an abstract logic is executed by a person at 11pm. That makes
it the right site for analysis and for intervention, without making it the
cause.

Whether that reply is sufficient is a live question, and I do not think it is
fully settled. Say so.
```

The objection with the most immediate consequences for Wednesday, so link it

Managing without refrigeration substitutes household labour and frequent
shopping for stored cold. Both are unevenly available, and week 2 already
established who absorbs additional domestic work when it appears.

Then land the connection to their pitches: this objection is exactly why the
two questions asked of every pitch on Wednesday are **who is this for** and
**what does it ask them to give up**. A proposal that only works for someone
with time, money and a car has answered an easier question than the one asked.

<div className="slide-head"><span>The constructive close</span><span>Weeks 7 &amp; 11</span></div>

## What a defensible cold future would need

<div className="def-rows" style={{ "--def-term-width": "17.5rem" }}>
  <div><b>Honest labels</b><p>What is known, what is not, and what to check.</p></div>
  <div><b>Ambient by default</b><p>Cold reserved for what genuinely needs it.</p></div>
  <div><b>Shared storage</b><p>Surplus moving sideways, not downwards into a bin.</p></div>
  <div><b>Shorter chains</b><p>Less for the cold chain to do.</p></div>
  <div><b>Returned competence</b><p>Judgement held by households, not rented.</p></div>
</div>

<p className="statement">None of these requires abolishing anything. Every one is a <strong>reallocation</strong>.</p>

```notes
**~2 min** (0:13)

The constructive close, assembled from weeks 7 and 11 rather than introduced
new. Point at where each came from — honest labels from week 7, the other
four from week 11's antidotes.

The last line is the one to emphasise: every item is a reallocation, not a
removal. Less refrigeration doing commercial work; the same or better
refrigeration doing the work that actually protects people. That is the
degrowth move applied to one appliance — a materially smaller system that
meets needs better, with no assumption that less throughput means less
wellbeing.

This slide is deliberately a **menu** rather than a programme. Their proposals
are the programme, and several will disagree with items on this list, which is
a good outcome and worth saying.
```

## One thing out

Pick a single item. Take it out of the fridge for a week. See what happens.

<div className="chain" style={{ "--cols": 5 }}>
  <span>Butter</span>
  <span>Bread</span>
  <span>Onions</span>
  <span>Unopened condiments</span>
  <span>Tomatoes</span>
</div>

<p className="statement">The boundary between “needs cold” and “doesn’t” is a <strong>hardened convention</strong>, not a physical fact.</p>

```notes
**~30 sec** (0:17)

The Unplugged Pledge from the session page, offered as an action rather than a
conclusion. Ten seconds; they can read the rest on the page.

**Tomatoes** are the one to name aloud if you name any — and they are the
marked link on this slide. Below about 12 °C the compounds responsible for
flavour stop being produced and the flesh goes mealy. Refrigeration is close
to the worst thing you can do to one. Easiest pledge on the list to keep, and
the most immediately convincing.

Eggs are the standing proof of the convention point — countries disagree
sharply, and the honest answer depends on where you live and how they were
processed. Do not oversell it.
```
